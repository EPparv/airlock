# Pair an iPhone with a Mac

1. Open Airlock on the Mac. Under **Privacy & connections → Pair iPhone**, unlock your personal workspace.
2. Choose **Start temporary link** and review the HTTPS provider disclosure, or choose **Use my HTTPS domain** to supply your own reverse proxy. If Airlock cannot confirm the temporary address from the Mac, scan the first code with your iPhone and type the eight digits shown in Safari back into the Mac. This checks the public address. The separate pairing step below controls access to records and actions. The bridge serves the phone and agent endpoints. The owner interface stays on the Mac.
3. If you want to try a real read, add a simple fact in **Context & memory → Memories**. Back in **Pair iPhone**, expand **Shared records**, select that fact and choose **Create pairing link**.
4. On the iPhone, scan the displayed code with Camera, or transfer the one-time link privately to Safari. Tap **Connect to Mac** within five minutes. Search for a word in your selected record. Safari shows the filtered text from your active Mac. No model is called by this phone view.
5. To try a write request, create a prepared action on the Mac under **Privacy & connections → Apps & data** and assign it to the paired iPhone connection. In Safari, tap **Check actions** and **Request Mac approval**. Use the Mac to approve or decline the exact action. The phone sees the owner-assigned label and status.
6. Tap **Disconnect this iPhone** or revoke the phone from the Mac’s **Pair iPhone** screen. Closing the tab discards the phone’s session key. Pair again to reconnect. The Mac must remain awake with the personal workspace open.

The link can be claimed once. Anyone who receives an unused link during its five-minute lifetime can claim it. The active grant allows up to 100 tool calls in one hour from creation. A user-selected HTTPS proxy, including the temporary tunnel provider, handles the phone’s TLS connection and can see released traffic. The Mac generates the QR locally. The session key stays in the phone tab’s memory and is cleared when the page closes. Data already read or copied on the phone cannot be recalled.

**Verification.** The Mac smoke check decodes the generated QR locally. The iOS Simulator WebKit test pairs through a trusted fixture HTTPS connection, reads an approved fictional memory, checks filtering, requests Mac approval for a fixture action and disconnects. No real mailbox or calendar is touched. The Python tests also exercise revocation, expiry, simultaneous claims, explicit browser origins, Mac workspace lock and relaunch, and denial of other tool endpoints.
